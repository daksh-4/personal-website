1 Hypersensitivity reactions in SLE

A 28-year-old woman with systemic lupus erythematosus presents to the emergency department with a painful, swollen left leg. Laboratory studies show a prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time with a normal bleeding time. Testing reveals antibodies against beta 2-glycoprotein I and cardiolipin. She has previously experienced two unexplained miscarriages. What type of hypersensitivity reaction is the underlying cause for the patient’s current presentation?

a. Type I b. Type II c. Type III d. Type IV

2 Retinal haemorrhages in the neonate

A 10-day-old boy is brought to the emergency department with irritability and poor feeding. Examination shows bilateral retinal haemorrhages and several ecchymoses on the trunk. There is also persistent bleeding from the umbilical stump. He was born at term following an uncomplicated pregnancy and is exclusively breastfed. Which of the following is the most likely explanation for his bleeding?

a. Deficiency of factor VIII
b. Maternal antibodies against platelet glycoproteins
c. Vitamin K deficiency
d. Disseminated intravascular coagulation
e. Shaken baby syndrome

3 Erythropoietin therapy

A 67-year-old man with stage 4 chronic kidney disease is found to have a haemoglobin concentration of 9.2 g/dL. His red blood cells are normocytic and normochromic. He is diagnosed with anaemia secondary to reduced erythropoietin production and is started on recombinant erythropoietin.

Several months later, he reports increasing fatigue, weakness and shortness of breath on exertion. Repeat blood tests show a haemoglobin concentration of 8.1 g/dL.

Which of the following is the most likely finding on his repeat blood film and red blood cell indices?

a. Normocytic, normochromic red blood cells with increased reticulocyte count
b. Normocytic, hypochromic red blood cells
c. Microcytic, normochromic red blood cells
d. Microcytic, hypochromic red blood cells
e. Macrocytic, normochromic red blood cells

4 Parkinsonism

A 34-year-old man with first-episode schizophrenia was started on haloperidol 3 weeks ago. He now has generalised stiffness, slowed movements and a mild resting tremor, which are symmetrical. His observations are normal and he is alert. Previous attempts to use alternative antipsychotics were not tolerated and the psychiatry team wish to continue haloperidol at the current dose.

Which of the following is the most appropriate mechanism of action to treat his movement symptoms?

a. Antagonism of muscarinic acetylcholine receptors
b. Increased dopamine synthesis by providing a dopamine precursor
c. Agonism of D2 dopamine receptors
d. Antagonism of β adrenergic receptors
e. Positive allosteric modulation of GABA-A receptors

5 Von Willebrand disease: screening results

A 22-year-old woman presents with a several year history menorrhagia, recurrent epistaxis and recently had prolonged bleeding after a dental extraction. Her father has a similar history. She is diagnosed with type 1 von Willebrand disease.

Which of the following sets of initial results is most likely?

Platelet count PT APTT Bleeding time
a. Normal Normal Prolonged Prolonged
b. Normal Prolonged Prolonged Normal
c. Low Normal Normal Prolonged
d. Normal Normal Prolonged Normal
e. Low Prolonged Prolonged Prolonged
